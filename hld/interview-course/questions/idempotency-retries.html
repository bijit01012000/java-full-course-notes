Idempotency and Safe Retries

Every network call can fail after the work succeeded, so retries are correct client behaviour and duplicates are a design assumption; interviewers want to see how you make the second attempt harmless.

One-line answerDuplicates are guaranteed, so make the effect idempotent, record the key atomically with the effect, and retry only transient failures with backoff, jitter, and a budget.

01Requirements

Functional
  • Accept retries of the same logical write with a client-supplied Idempotency-Key.
  • Return the original response for a completed key instead of executing the work again.
  • Reject a reused key that carries a different request payload.
  • Deduplicate queue messages by a producer-assigned event ID.
  • Retry transient failures with backoff and move poison messages to a dead-letter queue.
  • Expose the status of a key and support an audited DLQ replay.
Non-functional
  • At most one charge, order, refund, or notification per logical operation.
  • API p99 under 300 ms excluding the external provider call; retries must not run synchronously forever.
  • Idempotency lookup p99 under 10 ms on the happy path.
  • Retain idempotency and dedupe records longer than the maximum retry, redelivery, and replay horizon.
  • Fail closed for money movement and fail open for low-value analytics when the dedupe store is unavailable.
  • Retry budgets, jitter, and circuit breakers must prevent a retry storm from amplifying an outage.
  • Assume 500 payment writes/s at peak and a 5% retry rate, so about 25 retries/s arrive alongside new traffic.
  • A key record at about 500 bytes for 500 writes/s over 24 hours is about 43M rows and roughly 22 GB hot storage.
  • Keeping the same records for 7 days is roughly 150 GB, which is why the hot window is short and older records are archived.
  • A provider timeout of 2 s with at most 3 attempts and backoff of 100 ms, 500 ms, and 2 s caps one request's retry time.
  • If only failed attempts retry, a 5% failure rate adds about 5.25% attempts rather than multiplying all traffic by three.

04Data modelling

Every write checks a unique key record before acting, commits the effect and its record together, and publishes follow-on work through an outbox with a deduplicating consumer.
  • Name the duplicate sources first: client retries, gateway retries, queue redelivery, producer retries, replays, and a crash after the effect but before the acknowledgement.
  • Make the operation naturally idempotent where possible, for example an upsert or a status assignment, and use keys where the effect inherently accumulates.
  • The key record and the business effect must share one transaction; separating them creates a window for either a duplicate or a lost request.
  • The first request wins the unique insert and proceeds; a concurrent retry sees in-progress and either waits or returns a conflict, never starts a second execution.
  • Consumers acknowledge only after the effect and the dedupe row commit, so a crash causes redelivery that is now safe.
  • Retry only transient classes such as timeouts, connection resets, and 5xx; a 4xx business rejection will not succeed on attempt three.

06Optimisation & trade-offs

DecisionGainCost / risk
Idempotency keysA retry replays the original result instead of repeating the effectA lookup, a stored response, and a retention policy on every write path
Database-backed dedupeDurable and transactional with the effect, so a crash cannot separate themAdds write latency and the table grows with every request
Redis-backed dedupeVery fast lookup with native TTLEviction or a cache failure widens the duplicate window, so it cannot be the only record
Unique constraintRace-proof final barrier even when application checks raceOnly works when the business key has a natural uniqueness rule
Long dedupe windowCatches late retries, slow replays, and weekend backfillsMore storage, larger indexes, and higher lookup cost
Short dedupe windowSmall table and cheap lookupsA slow retry or replay can slip through and duplicate the effect
Fail-closed dedupe lookupPrevents a double charge when the store is unavailableTurns a dedupe-store outage into a payment-write outage
Dead-letter queueOne poison message cannot block every later event in the partitionRequires alerting, an audited replay path, and idempotent handlers on replay
  • Short-circuit a completed key before parsing the body or calling downstream services.
  • Use Redis as a fast negative or completed pre-check, but keep the durable table as the authoritative record.
  • Partition the idempotency and dedupe tables by key so they scale with traffic and do not become a single hot spot.
  • Store a hash of the request payload plus a compact response rather than the whole body, and compress large responses.
  • Schedule retries on a queue with a visible delay instead of sleeping a worker thread.
  • Apply exponential backoff with full jitter, a maximum attempt count, and a retry budget per dependency.
  • Add a circuit breaker so a failing provider stops receiving traffic instead of accumulating a retry storm.
  • Expire and archive dedupe records by partition, and alert on table growth as well as duplicate rate.

07Follow-ups — one-line answers

Why do duplicates happen at all? A client cannot distinguish a lost request from a lost response, so it retries; queues also redeliver after a crash, making duplicates an expected condition rather than an edge case.
What if the same key arrives with a different payload? Reject it with a conflict, because the key identifies one logical operation and silently returning the old response would hide a client bug.
Where should the key be stored? In the same database and transaction as the business effect when correctness matters, so a crash cannot leave one without the other.
How do you handle two concurrent retries? Use an atomic insert with a unique constraint on the key; one request wins and proceeds, while the other sees in-progress or completed and waits, rejects, or replays.
What happens if the process crashes between the effect and the key record? That is why the record and the effect share one transaction; if they cannot, the effect must be recoverable from an in-progress state with a lease, and success is never returned before the effect commits.
How long should records live? Longer than the maximum client retry, queue retention, and replay horizon, then archive or expire them to control storage.
Is exactly-once delivery achievable? Not end to end over an unreliable network; systems provide effectively-once effects through at-least-once delivery plus idempotent or deduplicated sinks.
How do you dedupe queue messages? Give each message a stable producer-assigned ID, write it to a processed table in the same transaction as the side effect, and acknowledge only after the commit.
What is a sane retry policy? Only retry transient errors, use exponential backoff with jitter and a maximum attempt count, cap retries with a budget, and stop calling a failing dependency behind a circuit breaker.
What do you do with a poison message? Bound the attempts, move it with its payload and error context to a dead-letter queue so the partition continues, alert on the oldest age, and replay through the same idempotent handler.
How do you avoid duplicate third-party charges? Pass an idempotency key to the provider when it supports one, record the send attempt locally, and reconcile the provider ledger against yours.
What should you monitor? Duplicate-detection rate by source, unique-constraint violations, retry rate and success after retry, DLQ volume and age, key-store size and eviction, and payment p99.

08Traps

  • Checking for a duplicate and then writing in two separate steps, leaving a race between concurrent retries.
  • Recording the idempotency key outside the transaction that performs the effect.
  • Generating a new key on every retry, which defeats the entire mechanism.
  • Deduplicating only at the API and forgetting queue redelivery and replays.
  • Claiming exactly-once delivery instead of explaining effectively-once processing.
  • Expiring dedupe records before the maximum retry or backfill window has passed.
