Design a News Feed

It is the cleanest test of fan-out: whether you can choose between push and pull, handle celebrity accounts, and keep a read-heavy feed fast while accepting bounded staleness.

One-line answerPush posts from ordinary accounts into per-user feed caches asynchronously, pull recent posts from celebrity accounts at read time, and hydrate a bounded page of post ids with cursor pagination.

01Requirements

Functional
  • Users can publish text and media posts, follow and unfollow other users, and delete or edit their own posts.
  • The home feed shows posts from followed accounts, newest first or ranked, with a stable cursor for pagination.
  • Feed reads include author, text, media, and engagement counts, and tolerate a few seconds of propagation delay.
  • A user sees their own new post immediately in their own feed.
  • Deleted posts and unfollowed accounts stop appearing within a bounded time.
  • Support a user profile timeline of posts by a single author.
Non-functional
  • Feed read p99 under a few hundred milliseconds, with a bounded amount of work per request regardless of how many accounts a user follows.
  • The feed is eventually consistent: a follower may see a new post seconds later, but never loses an older post because of fan-out lag.
  • Read traffic dominates; assume roughly 100 feed reads for every publish.
  • Stay available during a celebrity post or a traffic spike; one publish must not saturate the fan-out fleet.
  • Pagination must remain stable while new posts arrive, with no duplicates or skipped pages.
  • Assume 500M daily active users, each opening the feed 10 times a day: about 5B feed reads per day, roughly 58k per second average and 3x that at peak.
  • Assume 100M posts per day, about 1.2k publishes per second, with an average of 200 followers per author.
  • Naive write fan-out is therefore about 20B feed insertions per day, roughly 230k per second, which is why fan-out must be asynchronous and batched.
  • Cap each cached feed at 500-1000 post ids; a feed is a recent window, not a permanent archive.
  • At 500 ids and 8 bytes per id, caching 100M active feed readers is roughly 400GB of id storage before overhead, so cache only active users or trim aggressively.

02Key entities

Useruser_id, handle, display_name, avatar_url, follower_count, is_celebrity, status
Followfollower_id, followee_id, created_at (stored as both follower-to-followees and followee-to-followers)

03API design

MethodPathPurpose
POST/v1/postsCreate a post, persist it, and enqueue a fan-out event; returns as soon as the post is durable, not when fan-out completes.
GET/v1/feed?cursor=...&limit=20Return one page of hydrated feed items plus the next cursor, merging pushed entries with celebrity posts and the viewer's own posts.
POST/v1/users/{user_id}/followCreate a follow edge, update both adjacency lists, and optionally backfill a bounded number of recent posts.
DELETE/v1/users/{user_id}/followRemove a follow edge and stop that author's future posts from entering the follower's feed.
DELETE/v1/posts/{post_id}Soft-delete a post and mark it so cached feed entries and hydration skip it without rewriting every feed.
GET/v1/users/{user_id}/posts?cursor=...Return a single author's timeline directly from the post store for profile pages.

04Data modelling

DecisionChoiceWhy
Post storageShard posts by post_id for direct hydration, use a time-sortable post_id (Snowflake-style) as the ordering key, and keep a separate per-author timeline index for profile pages.Feed reads hydrate many authors' posts by id, so point lookups must not scatter across author shards; the author index serves profile timelines without making the main store author-partitioned.
Follow graphStore the edge twice: follower_id to followee_id for building a user's feed, and followee_id to follower_id for fan-out.Fan-out needs the follower list of an author, while feed repair and follow suggestions need the followee list of a user; a single direction makes one of those a full scan.
Feed cacheRedis sorted set per user keyed feed:{user_id}, score by post time and id, trimmed to the newest 500-1000 entries.A feed read becomes one bounded range query, id storage stays small, and trimming prevents inactive users from consuming memory forever.
Fan-out strategyHybrid: push posts from authors below a follower threshold, pull recent posts from authors above it and merge at read time.Pure push makes one celebrity post write to millions of feeds, while pure pull makes every read gather from every followee; hybrid bounds both the write and the read.
Post hydrationFetch post bodies in a batched multi-get from a shared post cache, falling back to the post database, and skip missing or deleted ids.The feed stores only ids, so many readers share one cached body; batching avoids one lookup per item and a missing body must not fail the whole page.
PaginationOpaque cursor encoding the last seen score and post_id, never an offset.New posts shift offsets and cause duplicates or skipped items; a cursor anchored to the last returned item stays stable as the feed changes.

05Architecture

Publishing writes the post durably and enqueues fan-out; workers push ids into ordinary followers' feed caches, while celebrity posts are merged and hydrated at read time.
  • The publish request writes the post, then writes a fan-out event to an outbox in the same transaction, and returns. A relay publishes the event to the queue so a crash between the database commit and the queue publish cannot lose fan-out.
  • Fan-out workers page through the follower list in batches rather than loading millions of ids into memory, and pipeline feed insertions per shard to keep throughput high.
  • Feed insertion must be idempotent by post_id, because the queue delivers at least once and a worker can retry after a timeout; duplicate ids in a sorted set collapse naturally, but counter updates need their own dedupe.
  • Mark authors above a follower threshold as high-fan-out. Do not push their posts. At read time, fetch their recent posts from a small per-author index and merge by score with the pushed feed.
  • Hydration is a separate service so the feed service never joins across the post database; it batches ids, filters deleted posts, and returns partial pages rather than failing on one missing item.
  • Engagement counts are cached aggregates updated asynchronously from like and comment events, so a feed read never runs a count query and a hot post does not concentrate write load on one row.
  • The author's own posts are merged into their feed at read time, which gives read-your-writes without waiting for fan-out.
  • Rebuild a feed from the follow graph and recent posts when its cache is lost; never treat the cache as the only copy of the feed.

06Optimisation & trade-offs

DecisionGainCost / risk
Fan-out on write for ordinary accountsFeed reads become a single bounded cache lookup, which keeps p99 low and makes capacity predictable.One publish becomes hundreds of writes, so the queue and worker fleet must absorb bursts and the system pays storage per recipient.
Fan-out on read for celebritiesOne post is stored once regardless of audience size, so a celebrity publish cannot saturate the worker fleet.Every reader of a celebrity must fetch and merge extra posts, and the read path becomes more complex and slightly slower.
Asynchronous fan-outPublishing stays fast and the queue absorbs spikes instead of making the author wait for millions of writes.Followers see posts seconds later, and the system needs lag monitoring, idempotent workers, and a dead-letter repair path.
Redis feed cache as the read modelFast ordered range reads and cheap trimming, with the database left to serve the durable post record.Cache loss requires a rebuild, memory is significant at scale, and the cache is eventually consistent with follows and deletes.
Cap feeds at 500-1000 entriesBounds memory and fan-out cost per user and matches how far back users realistically scroll.Older posts require a slower archive path, and a user returning after a long absence may see gaps until the feed is rebuilt.
Rank at read timeRanking can use fresh engagement signals and can be changed without rewriting every feed.Each read does more work and needs more candidate items than it returns; pure chronological feeds are cheaper and easier to explain.
Soft delete and filter at hydrationA delete is one row update instead of a fan-out to every feed that contains the post.Stale ids remain in caches and every read pays a small filtering cost; storage is not reclaimed immediately.
  • Cache the first page of each active user's feed separately so the most common request avoids a range query and hydration of a full page.
  • Batch hydrate 20-50 post ids per request and cache post bodies with a short TTL; hot posts are then served once for millions of readers.
  • Trim feeds to a fixed length and stop writing to users who have not opened the app recently; rebuild their feed on return.
  • Use cursor pagination with a deterministic tiebreaker of post_id so equal timestamps do not reorder between pages.
  • Coalesce duplicate fan-out work when an author posts several times in a row, and debounce feed invalidation for very active accounts.
  • Apply backpressure to the fan-out queue: if lag exceeds a threshold, pause low-priority fan-out and let reads fall back to pull rather than dropping posts.
  • Cache celebrity recent-post lists separately, since thousands of readers merge the same small list at the same time.
  • Filter deleted and blocked authors at hydration time, so deletion does not require rewriting millions of cached feeds.

07Follow-ups — one-line answers

How do you handle a celebrity with 50M followers? Do not push their posts; mark the account as high-fan-out and merge its recent posts from a per-author index at read time.
What happens when a user follows someone new? Write both edges, then backfill a bounded number of recent posts from the new followee rather than their entire history.
How does a deleted post disappear? Soft-delete the post and filter it during hydration, so cached feed ids become harmless without a mass rewrite.
How does the author see their own post immediately? Merge the author's recent posts into their feed at read time, giving read-your-writes without waiting for asynchronous fan-out.
Why cursor pagination instead of offset? New posts shift offsets and cause duplicates or skipped items, while a cursor anchored to the last item stays stable.
What if fan-out lags by minutes? Monitor queue age, pause low-priority work, and let affected reads fall back to pulling recent posts from followees until the backlog drains.
What if the feed cache loses a shard? Rebuild affected feeds from the follow graph and recent posts, serving a pull-based feed in the meantime rather than returning an empty page.
Where does ranking happen? Usually at read time over a bounded candidate set, because engagement signals change after publication and rewriting every feed for a ranking change is impractical.
How do you stop duplicate posts in a feed? Make fan-out insertion idempotent by post_id and deduplicate the merged push and pull candidates by id before hydration.
How do you handle a hot post read by millions? Cache the post body and its recent engagement counts close to the feed service, and coalesce cache misses so one request rebuilds the value.
Would you use push or pull for a brand-new user? Pull for an empty or cold feed, then switch to push once the user follows enough accounts and opens the app regularly.

08Traps

  • Fanning out synchronously in the publish request, which makes posting latency proportional to follower count and fails on celebrity accounts.
  • Using offset pagination on a feed that changes constantly, producing duplicate and skipped posts as users scroll.
  • Storing full post bodies in every user's feed, which multiplies storage and makes edits and deletes nearly impossible.
  • Choosing pure push or pure pull without discussing the follower distribution; neither extreme survives both ordinary users and celebrities.
  • Forgetting read-your-writes for the author, so a user posts and does not see their own post until fan-out finishes.
  • Treating the feed cache as the source of truth, with no rebuild path when a shard is lost or a fan-out job fails.
