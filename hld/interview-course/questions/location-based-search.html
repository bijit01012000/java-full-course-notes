Location-Based Search

It tests whether you can turn a two-dimensional proximity query into an indexable one, and whether you separate fast-moving live positions from slowly changing places.

One-line answerMap latitude and longitude to hierarchical cells, query the cell plus its neighbours, then filter by exact distance; keep live positions in a TTL cache and places in a spatial store.

01Requirements

Functional
  • Find places within a radius or the nearest k, filtered by category and open status.
  • Return distance plus the basic attributes needed to rank results.
  • Accept live location pings from drivers or couriers and expire stale ones.
  • Support viewport or bounding-box search for a map.
  • Track a trip by streaming position updates to the rider.
  • Optionally detect geofence enter and exit events.
Non-functional
  • Nearby query p99 under about 150 ms including ranking.
  • Live positions fresh within a few seconds, and stale entries must disappear.
  • Handle skewed density: dense city centres and empty rural cells.
  • Read-heavy, with a high write rate from moving objects.
  • Use haversine distance rather than flat degree arithmetic.
  • Coarsen or fuzz coordinates wherever privacy requires it.
  • Assume 50M static places and 300k active drivers pinging every 5 s, about 60k location writes/s at peak.
  • Nearby reads: roughly 30k/s average and 90k/s at peak.
  • A place document is about 500 B, so roughly 25 GB before indexes and replicas.
  • A live position record is under 200 B, so 300k records fit comfortably in memory.
  • At 5 s heartbeats, a 30 s TTL tolerates a few missed pings without showing ghosts.
  • A 1 km radius needs a handful of cells at moderate precision, and dense cells may return thousands of candidates, so cap the candidate set and rank.

04Data modelling

DecisionChoiceWhy
Spatial indexGeohash prefix at two or three precisions in a KV or search index, queried with the eight neighboursTurns proximity into a small set of range scans and prunes the world before exact distance.
Candidate fetchReturn IDs and lightweight fields first, then hydrate full documents only for the top resultsA dense cell can hold thousands of places, and hydrating all of them wastes I/O.
Distance filterBounding box prefilter, then haversine on the shortlistCell membership is not the same as being inside the radius, and degrees are not a flat grid.
Live positionsIn-memory geo store keyed by entity, overwritten on each ping, with a 30 s TTLOne small record per driver, low latency, and silent drivers disappear automatically.
HistoryAppend pings to a time-partitioned stream or table, separate from the live indexKeeps the hot index small while still supporting replay, ETA models, and audits.
ShardingShard by geohash prefix or city, duplicating border cells or querying adjacent shardsNearby queries stay local and one hot city does not fan out across the whole cluster.

Static places and live driver positions take separate paths: the nearby service reads cells and place details, then ranks, while ingest feeds a TTL cache and a durable stream.
  • Static places are read-mostly and live positions are write-heavy and ephemeral, so they get different stores.
  • The nearby path is a funnel: resolve the cell, fetch candidates, filter by exact distance, apply availability, rank, then limit.
  • Query the cell and its neighbours because a radius circle crosses rectangular cell boundaries.
  • Pick precision from the requested radius and local density, not one global constant.
  • Return last_seen or a freshness flag so the client can label stale results instead of presenting them as current.
  • Shard by geography, and handle borders by duplicating edge cells or merging results from adjacent shards.
  • If the spatial index is unavailable, fall back to a coarse cached viewport or a bounded database query, never a full scan.
  • Live tracking sends a snapshot on subscribe and then sequence-numbered updates; reconnect always resnapshots.

  • Cache viewport and popular pickup-point results for 5 to 30 s, but never cache live availability for long.
  • Fetch candidate IDs first and hydrate only the top results.
  • Cap radius and result count, and reject unbounded queries.
  • Batch and coalesce pings per device, and drop out-of-order updates using the sequence number.
  • Precompute the geohash at write time and store two or three precisions for different radii.
  • Use coarse cells for rural areas and finer cells for dense cities.
  • Serve places from read replicas and keep live positions in a separate in-memory cluster.
  • Throttle display updates for tracking and interpolate on the client.

07Follow-ups — one-line answers

Why can a normal B-tree index not answer a nearby query? A B-tree orders one dimension, so a radius predicate on both latitude and longitude scatters matches across the index; a spatial index maps both dimensions into cells so most data is pruned first.
How does a geohash work? It interleaves longitude and latitude bits and encodes them in base32, so a shared prefix identifies one rectangular cell and proximity becomes a prefix range scan.
Why query the eight neighbouring cells? The radius is a circle while the cell is a rectangle, so nearby points can sit just outside the user's cell and must be fetched before the exact distance filter.
How do you choose geohash precision? Match cell size to the requested radius and local density: too coarse returns too many candidates, too fine needs many cell lookups.
When would you use a quadtree instead? When density is highly uneven and the index can live in memory, because a quadtree subdivides crowded regions further, at the cost of splits and merges as objects move.
How do you store live driver locations? In a fast in-memory spatial store keyed by driver, overwriting the latest position, with a TTL, while a separate durable stream keeps history.
How do you avoid showing a driver who went offline? Expire records with a TTL, remove them explicitly on disconnect, and check the last heartbeat at read time, returning freshness with the result.
How do you handle the antimeridian and the poles? Normalize longitude wrapping and split a query that crosses the antimeridian into two ranges; near the poles use a spherical cell system because rectangles distort badly.
What about a stadium or a hot city cell? Split dense cells into finer sub-cells, add replicas for the hot partition, pre-aggregate results, and cap candidate counts.
How do you scale across cities? Shard by city or cell so nearby queries stay local, replicate hot partitions, and resolve border queries by duplicating edge cells or querying adjacent shards.
What are the privacy concerns? Exact coordinates reveal home and routines, so coarsen public locations, restrict access, set retention limits, and keep raw trails out of broad analytics.
What would you monitor? Query latency by cell, candidate-set size, stale heartbeat rate, hot-cell skew, update lag, and the share of queries falling back to a degraded path.

08Traps

  • Computing distance to every row and assuming it scales because the dataset is small today.
  • Using a geohash prefix query without fetching the neighbouring cells.
  • Treating latitude and longitude as a flat Cartesian grid everywhere.
  • Indexing only latitude and expecting the longitude predicate to be cheap.
  • Leaving stale drivers visible because there is no TTL or disconnect cleanup.
  • Sharding by geography without planning for hot cities and cross-boundary queries.
